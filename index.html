Tiny greens. Big nutrition.

Microgreens are young plants cut in 7 to 14 days. They are small, but full of vitamins and antioxidants.

Meet the greens

Why microgreens?

Most people have never heard of them. Here’s why they deserve a place on your plate.

Packed with nutrients

Gram for gram, they can hold more nutrients than the full-grown plant.

Grow at home

One tray on your kitchen shelf is enough. No garden needed.

Ready in a week

Most are ready to cut in 6 to 10 days.

Easy to eat

Add them to salad, sandwiches, dosa or rice.

Five greens to know

Each one has its own taste and its own strengths.

Good for everyday health

Microgreens are low in carbs and calories, and rich in fibre and antioxidants. That makes them a smart add-on for diabetes and PCOS meals.

Made for busy days

Healthy food only works if it is easy. This one is.

How much should you eat?

25–30 g

A small handful a day. New to microgreens? Start with 10 to 15 g. Wheatgrass: 30 ml fresh juice. (A general guide, not a fixed dose.)

Eat raw or add after cooking. Heat reduces some nutrients.
